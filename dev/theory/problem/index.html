The portfolio problem

This page fixes the notation used in the Theory pages and states the one property that every formulation of the package relies on.

Notation

SymbolMeaningIn the code
$N$number of assetsnum_assets(market)
$w \in \mathbb{R}^N$money invested in each asset (the weights when the budget is 1)the decision variables w
$r \in \mathbb{R}^N$random returns of the assets over the next period
$R = r^\top w$random return of the portfolio

$\Sigma \succ 0$ (positive definite) is assumed wherever a Cholesky factor $\Sigma^{1/2}$ (with $\Sigma = \Sigma^{\top/2} \Sigma^{1/2}$) is used.

The problem

A PortfolioFormulation with objective terms $(\rho_j, c_j)$ (ObjectiveTerm) and risk constraints $(\rho_i, \mathcal{C}_i)$ (RiskConstraint) is the problem

\[\begin{aligned} \max_{w} \quad & \sum_j c_j \, \rho_j(w) \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & \rho_i(w) \in \mathcal{C}_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, m, \\ & w \in \mathcal{X}, \end{aligned}\]

(or $\min$ with MIN_SENSE), where each $\mathcal{C}_i$ is $(-\infty, b]$ (LessThan(b)), $[b, \infty)$ (GreaterThan(b)) or $\{b\}$ (EqualTo(b)). portfolio_model! adds the terms and constraints to a JuMP model whose variables $w$ and constraints $w \in \mathcal{X}$ were created by market_model.

Conic representation of the measures

Most measures are not affine in $w$: a worst case, a tail expectation, a norm. For each measure, calculate_measure! adds auxiliary variables $u$ and conic constraints $(w, u) \in \mathcal{K}$ (linear, second order, exponential or semidefinite cones) to the model, and returns an affine expression $f(w, u)$. The measures fall in two classes:

  • concave measures (returns, worst cases, utilities), for which

    \[\rho(w) = \max_{u \,:\, (w, u) \in \mathcal{K}} f(w, u),\]

    so $f(w, u) \leq \rho(w)$ for every feasible $u$;
  • convex measures (variance, standard deviation, norms), for which

    \[\rho(w) = \min_{u \,:\, (w, u) \in \mathcal{K}} f(w, u),\]

    so $f(w, u) \geq \rho(w)$ for every feasible $u$.
Exactness and the direction of optimization

The reformulation of a measure is exact when the optimization pushes $f$ in the direction of its class: a concave measure is maximized (positive weight with MAX_SENSE, negative weight with MIN_SENSE) or constrained from below (GreaterThan); a convex measure is minimized or constrained from above (LessThan).

In that case, any optimal solution of the conic problem gives a portfolio $w$ that is optimal for the original problem and satisfies every risk constraint evaluated on the measure itself (not only on its reformulation), and the optimal values coincide.

In the other direction the problem is unbounded or a relaxation: for example a concave measure with an EqualTo(b) constraint only enforces $\rho(w) \geq b$. This is the usual convexity requirement: maximizing a convex function, or constraining a concave function from above, is not a convex problem.

The Risk measures and Ambiguity sets pages give, for each measure, its definition, its class, its conic reformulation and the conditions under which the reformulation is exact.
