Ambiguity sets

When the distribution of the returns is not known, a measure can be evaluated in the worst case over a set of possible means (robust optimization) or of possible distributions (distributionally robust optimization). Every set is a CenteredAmbiguitySet around a nominal distribution d (distribution), with mean $\hat{r}$ and covariance $\Sigma$. Measures that only use the covariance (Variance, SqrtVariance) use the covariance of d.

All worst cases are concave measures (see the direction of optimization): they are exact when maximized or constrained from below. Each reformulation is the dual of the inner worst case problem, so the auxiliary variables are dual variables and no inner problem is solved.

Budget uncertainty set

\[\mathcal{U}_\Gamma = \left\{ \mu : \mu_i = \hat{r}_i + z_i \Delta_i, \; |z_i| \leq 1, \; \textstyle\sum_i |z_i| \leq \Gamma \right\}, \qquad \rho(w) = \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{U}_\Gamma} \mu^\top w .\]

$\Delta \geq 0$ is the maximum deviation of each mean and $\Gamma \in [0, N]$ the number of means that can deviate. $\Gamma = 0$ is the nominal mean and $\Gamma = N$ the box of Soyster (1973). The worst case is a linear program; calculate_measure! adds the constraints of its dual and returns the dual objective (see the docstring of BudgetSet).

By linear programming duality, for every portfolio, including short positions, the dual expression equals the primal worst case, and both equal the greedy closed form

\[\rho(w) = \hat{r}^\top w - \sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor \Gamma \rfloor} g_{(k)} - (\Gamma - \lfloor \Gamma \rfloor) \, g_{(\lfloor \Gamma \rfloor + 1)}, \qquad g_i = \Delta_i |w_i| \text{ sorted decreasingly.}\]

Elliptical uncertainty set

\[\mathcal{U}_\delta = \left\{ \mu : \lVert \Sigma^{-1/2} (\mu - \hat{r}) \rVert_2 \leq \delta \right\}, \qquad \rho(w) = \min_{\mu \in \mathcal{U}_\delta} \mu^\top w = \hat{r}^\top w - \delta \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2 .\]

For $\delta \geq 0$ and $\Sigma \succ 0$, the minimizer is $\mu^\star = \hat{r} - \delta \Sigma w / \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2$ (Cauchy–Schwarz), so the second order cone reformulation equals the closed form.

Moment uncertainty (Delage & Ye)

The distributions whose first two moments are close to the nominal ones, and whose support is in a box $[\underline{r}, \overline{r}]$ (by default $\mathbb{R}^N$):

\[\mathcal{P}(\gamma_1, \gamma_2) = \left\{ P : \; \begin{aligned} & (\mathbb{E}_P[r] - \hat{r})^\top \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbb{E}_P[r] - \hat{r}) \leq \gamma_1, \\ & \mathbb{E}_P[(r - \hat{r})(r - \hat{r})^\top] \preceq \gamma_2 \Sigma, \\ & P(\underline{r} \leq r \leq \overline{r}) = 1 \end{aligned} \right\}.\]

For a concave piecewise linear function $h(r) = \min_k (a_k r^\top w + b_k)$ of the returns, the worst case expectation $\inf_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[h(r)]$ is the negative of the optimal value of the semidefinite program below, an equivalent form of Delage & Ye (2010, Lemma 1)

\[\begin{aligned} \min_{Q, q, r_0, P, p, t} \quad & \gamma_2 \langle \Sigma, Q \rangle - \hat{r}^\top Q \hat{r} + r_0 + \langle \Sigma, P \rangle - 2 \hat{r}^\top p + \gamma_1 t \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & p = -q/2 - Q \hat{r}, \quad \begin{bmatrix} P & p \\ p^\top & t \end{bmatrix} \succeq 0, \\ & \xi^\top Q \xi + \xi^\top (q + a_k w) + r_0 + b_k \geq 0 \quad \forall \xi \in [\underline{r}, \overline{r}], \; \forall k. \end{aligned}\]

With unbounded support, the last constraint is exactly $\begin{bmatrix} Q & (q + a_k w)/2 \\ (q + a_k w)^\top/2 & r_0 + b_k \end{bmatrix} \succeq 0$. With a box support, each finite bound gets a nonnegative multiplier $\lambda$ that is added to the linear term (Yang et al., 2014), which is a sufficient condition for the constraint to hold on the box. The measures over this set are:

  • ExpectedReturn: $h(r) = r^\top w$. With unbounded support it equals $\hat{r}^\top w - \sqrt{\gamma_1} \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2$.
  • ConditionalExpectedReturn: minus the worst case CVaR, $\max_z \; z - \frac{1}{\alpha} \sup_{P} \mathbb{E}_P[(z - r^\top w)^+]$, with $-(z - r^\top w)^+ = \min(0, r^\top w - z)$. With unbounded support and $\gamma_1 = 0$ it equals $\hat{r}^\top w - \sqrt{\gamma_2 (1 - \alpha) / \alpha} \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2$. $\alpha$ has to be in $(0, 1]$.
  • ExpectedUtility with a PieceWiseUtility.

The set is defined for $\gamma_1 \geq 0$, $\gamma_2 \geq 1$, $\Sigma \succ 0$ and $\underline{r} \leq \hat{r} \leq \overline{r}$; with $\gamma_2 \geq 1$ it contains the nominal distribution. Some consequences:

  • With unbounded support the reformulation is exact (Delage & Ye, 2010) and gives the closed forms above.
  • With a box support the value is a lower bound of the exact worst case over the box, and it is never smaller than the value with unbounded support.
  • The nominal distribution is in the set, so the worst case expected return is at most $\hat{r}^\top w$ for every portfolio, and the optimal worst case objective is at most the optimal nominal objective.
  • The sets are nested in $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$, so the worst case is nonincreasing in both.
  • The Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\hat{r}, \Sigma)$ is in the set, so the worst case conditional expected return is at most its Gaussian value $\hat{r}^\top w - \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2 \, \phi(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)) / \alpha$.

Wasserstein ball

DuWassersteinBall is the ball of radius $\epsilon$ (type-1 Wasserstein distance) around the empirical distribution $\hat{P}_S$ of the samples $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_S$, with a support bound $\Lambda$ and a scaling matrix $Q$ (default $I$). The worst case expected return is reformulated following Du et al. (2021, Corollaries 1–3) as

\[\begin{aligned} \max_{\lambda, s, \nu, \tau} \quad & -\left(\lambda \epsilon + \frac{1}{S} \sum_{i=1}^S s_i\right) \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & -w^\top \xi_i + \nu_i^\top Q^{-1} \xi_i + \Lambda \tau_i \leq s_i, \\ & \lVert w - Q^{-1} \nu_i \rVert_p \leq \lambda, \quad \lVert \nu_i \rVert_q \leq \tau_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, S, \end{aligned}\]

where $p \in \{1, 2, \infty\}$ is norm_cone and $q$ its dual exponent (a new $p$ can be added with a method of PortfolioOpt.primal_cone).

The reformulation needs $\epsilon \geq 0$, $\Lambda$ at least the largest norm of a sample (the default) and $Q$ invertible. No closed form is available in general, but with $\epsilon = 0$ the ball is the empirical distribution, so the value is the sample mean return; the balls are nested in $\epsilon$, so the value is nonincreasing in $\epsilon$ and at most the sample mean return, for $p = 1, 2, \infty$.
