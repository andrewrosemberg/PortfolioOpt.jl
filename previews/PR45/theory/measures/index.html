Risk measures

This page defines each measure over a distribution of the returns (a Distributions.Sampleable or DeterministicSamples); worst cases over ambiguity sets are on the Ambiguity sets page. The notation is defined in The portfolio problem. Every measure is a subtype of PortfolioRiskMeasure, and its reformulation is added to the model by calculate_measure!.

"Direction" means exact when optimized in the direction of its class. s stands for DeterministicSamples, and d for any distribution with a mean and a covariance (for the exponential utility: MvNormal, a MixtureModel of MvNormals, or DeterministicSamples).

Expected return

\[\rho(w) = \mathbb{E}_P[r^\top w] = \hat{r}^\top w .\]

An affine expression: exact in any direction. Over an ambiguity set it is the worst case expected return (see Ambiguity sets).

Variance and standard deviation

\[\mathrm{Var}(R) = w^\top \Sigma w, \qquad \sigma(R) = \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2 .\]

  • Variance of plain variables (or numbers) returns the quadratic expression $w^\top \Sigma w$. Of other JuMP expressions it returns an epigraph variable $v$ with $(v, 1/2, \Sigma^{1/2} w)$ in the rotated second order cone, i.e. $v \geq w^\top \Sigma w$.
  • SqrtVariance returns $t$ with $(t, \Sigma^{1/2} w)$ in the second order cone, i.e. $t \geq \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} w \rVert_2$.

The covariance must be positive definite, $\Sigma \succ 0$, since a Cholesky factorization gives $\Sigma^{1/2}$. Over an ambiguity set, the covariance of its nominal distribution is used.

Both epigraphs are exact in the convex direction: minimized, they equal $w^\top \Sigma w$ and its square root, so a portfolio optimal with RiskConstraint(SqrtVariance(d), LessThan(σ)) satisfies $\sqrt{w^\top \Sigma w} \leq σ$.

Worst case return

For samples $r_1, \dots, r_S$,

\[\rho(w) = \min_{s} r_s^\top w = \max_{\theta} \{ \theta : \theta \leq r_s^\top w, \; s = 1, \dots, S \}.\]

At the optimum the linear reformulation equals the smallest sample return. This is the worst case over the convex hull of the samples (Fernandes et al., 2016).

Conditional expected return (CVaR)

The expected return over the worst $\alpha$ fraction of outcomes, $\alpha \in (0, 1]$, i.e. minus the conditional value at risk (expected shortfall) of the return. Rockafellar & Uryasev (2000) show that

\[-\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(R) = \max_{z \in \mathbb{R}} \; z - \frac{1}{\alpha} \mathbb{E}\left[(z - R)^+\right],\]

which for samples is the linear program

\[\begin{aligned} \max_{z, y} \quad & z - \frac{1}{\alpha} \sum_{s=1}^{S} p_s y_s \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & y_s \geq z - r_s^\top w, \quad y_s \geq 0, \quad s = 1, \dots, S. \end{aligned}\]

For samples sorted so that $R_{(1)} \leq \dots \leq R_{(S)}$ and $K = \lfloor \alpha S \rfloor$, its value is the tail mean

\[-\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(R) = \frac{1}{\alpha} \left( \frac{1}{S} \sum_{k=1}^{K} R_{(k)} + \left(\alpha - \frac{K}{S}\right) R_{(K+1)} \right),\]

the mean of the $\alpha S$ worst returns when $\alpha S$ is an integer. α = 0 is the worst case return, and α = 1 the expected return.

The tail-mean formula is for samples with equal probabilities and $\alpha \in [0, 1]$. For every $z$, $z - \mathbb{E}[(z - R)^+]/\alpha \leq -\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(R)$, so every feasible point of the linear program is a conservative estimate, and its optimal value is the tail mean above, also when $\alpha S$ is not an integer (Rockafellar & Uryasev, 2000). Hence a portfolio optimal with RiskConstraint(ConditionalExpectedReturn(s; α), GreaterThan(b)) has a sample tail mean of at least $b$.

Exponential utility and certainty equivalent

The exponential (constant absolute risk aversion) utility with risk aversion $a > 0$ is

\[u(x) = \frac{1 - e^{-a x}}{a},\]

a positive affine transformation of $-e^{-ax}$. Both measures depend on $M(w) = \mathbb{E}[e^{-a R}]$:

\[\mathbb{E}[u(R)] = \frac{1 - M(w)}{a}, \qquad CE(R) = u^{-1}(\mathbb{E}[u(R)]) = -\frac{1}{a} \log M(w).\]

The certainty equivalent is minus the entropic risk measure, in units of return. $M(w)$ is a sum of exponentials of convex functions of $w$:

  • Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\hat{r}, \Sigma)$: $M(w) = \exp(-a \hat{r}^\top w + \tfrac{a^2}{2} w^\top \Sigma w)$, with an epigraph $v \geq w^\top \Sigma w$ (rotated SOC);
  • Gaussian mixture with probabilities $\pi_k$: $M(w) = \sum_k \pi_k \exp(-a \hat{r}_k^\top w + \tfrac{a^2}{2} w^\top \Sigma_k w)$ (Luxenberg & Boyd, 2024);
  • samples: $M(w) = \sum_s p_s \exp(-a r_s^\top w)$.

The expected utility uses one exponential cone $t_k \geq e^{e_k}$ per term; the certainty equivalent is the hypograph $\theta \leq -\frac{1}{a} \log \sum_k \pi_k e^{e_k}$, written as $\sum_k \pi_k e^{e_k + a \theta} \leq 1$. For a Gaussian, $CE = \hat{r}^\top w - \frac{a}{2} w^\top \Sigma w$, so maximizing it is the mean-variance problem with risk aversion $a$.

The Gaussian terms need $\Sigma \succ 0$, and the solver must support exponential cones (e.g. Clarabel, SCS, Mosek). Since $u$ is concave, Jensen's inequality gives $\mathbb{E}[u(R)] \leq u(\mathbb{E}[R])$ and $CE(R) \leq \mathbb{E}[R]$. Both reformulations are exact in the concave direction, so a certainty equivalent constraint GreaterThan(b) holds for the closed form at the optimal portfolio.

Norm regularizers

ConeRegularizer(K, M) is $t$ with $(t, M w) \in K$ for a norm cone $K$ (e.g. MOI.NormOneCone(N + 1), MOI.SecondOrderCone(N + 1)), i.e. $t \geq \lVert M w \rVert$. With a negative weight in a maximization (a penalty) it is exact: at the optimum $t = \lVert M w \rVert$.
